EU Energy Transition (2000-2025)

Tracking the EU-27 energy transition. Data: Our World in Data energy dataset. Charts: D3.js

Primary Energy Consumption (2024)

Total primary energy demand across the EU-27, alongside per-capita use. Demand is heavily concentrated: Germany, France, Italy, Spain and Poland account for % of the EU total, with Germany alone at 3,195 TWh.

Switch the map to per capita and the ranking flips entirely: Malta, Finland and Luxembourg sit on top, while Romania uses roughly a quarter of Malta's level.

* Malta data is from 2023

Wind Energy Producers (2025)

Wind is the EU's biggest source of renewable electricity, and it is Germany's race: 136 TWh in 2025, more than Spain and France, the next two, combined. The top three alone produce roughly half of the EU's ~480 TWh of wind power.

Geography shows through: the North Sea and Baltic coastlines fill the front of the field, while Slovakia, Slovenia and Malta generate essentially none. Even so, the global race is elsewhere: China alone generates about twice as much wind power as the entire EU.

Solar Energy Producers (2025)

Solar is spread more evenly than wind, but the podium looks familiar: Germany (90 TWh), Spain (63) and Italy (45) deliver over half of the EU's ~367 TWh. Bubble size represents energy produced.

Renewables Over the Years

How each of the four renewable sources grew from 2000 to 2025: a wind turbine per year (tower height = wind generation), a sun per year (height = solar generation), and lines for hydro and biofuels.

How much of each country's total energy demand, not just electricity, is met by renewables, split by source: solar, wind, hydro, biofuels, and other renewables (geothermal, wave, tidal). Sweden leads at 51%; Czechia still sits at just 10%.

The composition matters as much as the total: Denmark's 42% is built on wind, hydro carries Sweden, Austria and Latvia, and biofuels do surprisingly heavy lifting across the Baltics and Nordics.

Malta is excluded: it reports no renewable-share data. Shares run through 2024, the latest reported year.

Renewable Energy Share of Primary Energy (2024)

The electricity mix since 2000. Across the EU-27, wind and solar have grown from almost nothing to nearly a third of all generation by 2025, while coal's share has more than halved.

Filter to a country to see how different the national systems are: nuclear-anchored France, renewables-led Germany, gas-reliant Italy, coal-heavy Poland.

Electricity Generation by Source

Absolute electricity generation from coal versus renewables, in TWh. The EU-wide lines crossed around 2012-2013 and the gap has since exploded: by 2025 renewables deliver roughly 1,300 TWh, more than five times coal.

Even lignite-heavy Germany crossed over in 2019, while Poland, the last big holdout, still leans on coal for most of its electricity.

Coal vs. Renewables (Electricity Generation)

GDP versus primary energy use, indexed to 2000 = 100. The EU-27 economy grew ~48% while energy consumption fell ~10%: the definition of absolute decoupling.

* GDP is adjusted for inflation and differences in living costs between countries, and is only available through 2022.
